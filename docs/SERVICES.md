# Services, weather and privacy details

## Weather

Choose **Departure** in **Trip options**: once the route is ready, weather is requested automatically after a brief pause. Without a departure you choose, the route uses an **automatic departure**: the next half hour in the device timezone (10:07 becomes 10:30), which moves forward every 30 minutes while you plan, so weather is requested from Open-Meteo as soon as the route is ready and again at each new slot (not while the tab is hidden). It is never stored in links, saved routes or backups. Editing or clearing the field fixes the choice and unchecks **Automatic departure** below it; checking it again returns to the next half hour; unchecking it by hand turns it off on this device (`auto-departure` in localStorage), and then there is no weather request without a date and Weather asks for a departure. Input and all displayed times use the device timezone, including daylight saving changes. Departures in the last **90 UTC calendar days** are accepted; older departures or arrivals beyond the forecast horizon are rejected. For past dates, values are archived model estimates, not exact observations at the route point. Departure accompanies links and saved routes with backward compatibility; reopening requests weather again without storing the forecast. Failures offer **Try again**, without automatic retries. Weather and elevation share one budget per page (599 coordinates a minute, 4,999 an hour, 9,999 a day, 299,999 in 31 days); when it is spent, or Open-Meteo answers HTTP 429, weather and elevation show a countdown ("Try again in 30s.", from `Retry-After` when the response exposes it, otherwise 60 s) and then retry once on their own; refused again, the Try again button appears when the next countdown ends.

The complete track, including imported GPX, determines cumulative distance. Samples are approximately every 20 km, including start, destination and waypoints, spaced evenly within each leg between route points (a 15.3 km leg gets samples every 3.8 km at 5 km/h), so no sample lands right beside a route point and no gap exceeds the spacing while the cap allows; moving a route point only changes the samples of its neighbouring legs. Below 20 km/h, spacing is reduced to about one hour of travel (for example, 5 km at 5 km/h). Points left free under the maximum go to sharp bends (`routeBends` in `src/weather.ts`: a turn of at least 60° between the 300 m before and after a point, checked every 100 m, one per kilometre, never across GPX segment gaps), largest turn first, placed 100 m past the turn and at least 2 km (or a quarter of the spacing, if larger) from other samples, so the wind relation follows the new direction; distance samples are never displaced. The maximum of 20 points (so at most 20 cards and 20 map markers) takes priority, so long routes or many waypoints can require larger gaps. With more than 20 distinct route points, start and destination are kept and the other samples are the stops closest to 18 evenly spaced targets along the route, preferring named stops over automatic stops of an imported track; the panel shows "Weather sampled at N of M stops." Passage times use your **Average speed** (60 km/h until you change it), the same speed as the route time, arrival and daylight; the route summary shows it in km/h or mph. It can be adjusted for slow or fast travel and is not tied to a vehicle. Stops and traffic are excluded from calculation. Speed changes recalculate locally while the fetched interval covers the window; if exceeded, use the refresh option. Slower travel automatically requests additional points when needed for the shorter spacing; faster travel reuses the existing points without another request. Track, departure or server changes update the forecast automatically. Each sample has one card combining time, distance, conditions and risks; consecutive samples with equivalent conditions (same description, severity and alerts, wind relation, UV category and pass, temperature within 2 °C, precipitation probability within 20 points and mean wind within 10 km/h of the first sample of the run) share one card and one map marker, with time, distance and value ranges, while start, stops and destination always keep their own. Cards use outline SVG icons on cards and colored, aligned versions on the map. Levels 1, 2 and 3 use yellow, orange and red plus an alert label; cards without alerts are neutral. All remain visible without inner scrolling. Selecting a weather icon opens the panel and focuses its matching forecast card, without a map popup. Selecting a card centers the map on that point; on mobile it also collapses the panel. Cards work with Enter and Space. Wind has an icon and is shown as headwind, tailwind or crosswind relative to the local track direction, with speed in the selected units. The nearest forecast hour is used: within 45° of wind coming from ahead is headwind, within 45° of behind is tailwind, otherwise crosswind; below 1 km/h is calm. The outgoing edge is used at bends and the incoming edge at the destination. This is model wind at 10 m, not apparent wind or a traffic/speed-dependent resistance calculation; local shelter and terrain can change it.

Map markers also show wind speed and an arrow pointing **toward where the wind blows**. Marker colors indicate headwind (red), tailwind (green), crosswind (amber), or calm/unavailable (gray). Relative direction is also named in the cards and accessible labels. Accessible labels classify mean wind: calm <1 km/h, light 1–<20, moderate 20–<39, strong 39–<62 and very strong ≥62. These simplified [Beaufort boundaries](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/general-marine-weather-information/understanding-forecasts/beaufort-wind-scale-table.html) are display categories, separate from the gust alert threshold of 60 km/h.

Source: [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs), configurable endpoint `https://api.open-meteo.com`, HTTPS/CORS and no key. Terms and documentation checked on 2026-11-08: the free API is **for non-commercial use only**, without an availability guarantee. Published limits: 600 calls/minute, 5,000/hour, 10,000/day and 300,000/month; keep usage below these ceilings. One request groups latitude/longitude lists, but **each location counts**; more than 10 variables or more than two weeks increases the count, including fractional calls. Eight hourly variables are used: temperature, precipitation probability and volume, weather code, wind speed/direction, gusts and visibility. See the [terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms), [call accounting](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing) and [license](https://open-meteo.com/en/licence).

The future horizon is **16 UTC calendar days, including today**, with the last **90 days** available through the same endpoint using explicit hourly dates; departure, arrival and the one-hour margin must fit available hours. One request covers departure to arrival plus margin, rounding coverage to the end of the UTC day to allow local adjustments. Requests are serialized, at least six seconds between starts; a conservative in-memory budget also checks hourly, daily and monthly limits. The ten-minute memory cache holds up to 200 rounded locations and 100,000 hourly entries per page. Covered locations are reused even when their order or the route changes; only missing distinct locations are grouped in a new request. Cache hits do not extend freshness. There are no automatic retries. These per-page limits cannot control aggregate traffic across tabs, devices or the project; stop or reconfigure use before scaling.

Data is [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/): the interface displays “Weather data by Open-Meteo”, the license link and notice that passage times and risk summaries are calculated by route-gpx. The data license allows reuse with attribution but does not remove the free API's non-commercial restriction.

Risk classification is a review aid, not an official warning, taking the worst case within ±1 hour (hourly bounds rounded outward). Thresholds: rain risk at probability ≥60% or volume ≥1 mm/h; heavy rain ≥10 mm/h; thunderstorms from codes 95/96/99; fog from codes 45/48 or visibility ≤1,000 m; gusts ≥60 km/h; cold/possible ice ≤2 °C or freezing codes; extreme heat ≥40 °C; very high UV index ≥8 and extreme ≥11 (WHO categories, shown on daytime cards from Open-Meteo `uv_index`, the preceding-hour mean; a missing UV series does not fail the forecast). Alerts appear by severity and missing data is flagged. Forecasts are approximate, on grids of several kilometres, can miss local showers and become less reliable further ahead. Passage times depend on traffic, speed and stops; real forecast accuracy cannot be evaluated here. GPX remains identical, without point timestamps.

Change service base URLs in **Settings**. Settings keep server URLs in two places by design: the OSRM, Valhalla and Nominatim servers belong to the route, so they travel with route links (only when they differ from the defaults), the JSON editor, saved routes and backups; the Photon, Open-Meteo and IP-region servers are preferences of this device, kept in localStorage and never included in links, saved routes or backups. Route links carry routing and search servers only when they differ from the defaults; a link that suggests other servers opens with the defaults, and a notice names the suggested hosts. Nothing is sent to them unless you choose **Use link servers**, so a crafted link cannot redirect your requests. Remote services need HTTPS and must allow browser cross-origin requests. For self-hosting, consult the [OSRM documentation](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend#quick-start) and [Nominatim documentation](https://nominatim.org/release-docs/latest/admin/Installation/).

The compact **i** button at the bottom right opens a modal with the active layer's full map credits and license links, plus weather credits. Links open in a new tab; **Close**, Escape or a tap outside the card closes it and returns focus to **i**. Changing layers closes the modal and updates its contents. On small screens, the route panel covers the control while open; close the panel to access it.

The Map layers control loads layers only when active; satellite Labels are optional and initially enabled. The selection and satellite edition are device preferences, outside route links and saved routes. The active tile provider receives your IP address and visible-area tile requests; labels send additional visible-area requests to EOX. No tile prefetching, offline storage or service worker is added. Zoom beyond native resolution enlarges existing tiles; it adds no detail.

## Elevation

Elevation is requested only when **Elevation** is opened in the route summary or the **Grade** route coloring is chosen in Map layers (the same samples, cache and limits; the coloring classes the smoothed profile at ±3 % and ±8 % over 500 m of terrain or 100 m of recorded elevation). An imported GPX, TCX or FIT file whose track points record elevation (`<ele>`, `AltitudeMeters` or FIT altitude; at least 80% of them) is used locally with no request. Otherwise the route is sampled evenly within each leg between route points, restarting at every point, with a spacing from a fixed ladder (100 m × 2^(k/8)) chosen to keep about 300 points at most (plus leg and segment ends). Editing a point changes only the samples of the legs next to it, so the rest come from the cache, and an open Elevation waits 2 s for a recalculated route to settle before requesting and sent to the Open-Meteo [elevation API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/elevation-api) at the configured Open-Meteo server, in serialized requests of up to 100 rounded coordinates, without automatic retries. Values stay in a page-memory cache (5,000 locations, ten minutes). Data comes from the [Copernicus DEM GLO-90](https://doi.org/10.5270/ESA-c5d3d65) (about 90 m), attributed in the map credits; bridges, viaducts and tunnels show terrain, not the road. Terrain elevation passes an 850 m median filter and gain/loss ignore changes under 2 m; recorded elevation passes a five-point median and ignores changes under 0.3 m. These values were calibrated against recorded activities on hilly and rolling routes. Climbs are detected locally from the same smoothed profile (no extra requests): at least 40 m gain, 400 m length and 2.5% average, split by drops over 15 m or 1.5 km without gaining; the steepest grade uses 500 m windows for terrain and 100 m for recorded elevation, and a terrain climb steeper than 15% over 500 m is flagged as a likely tunnel or bridge. ## Route steps

Calculated routes keep, for each step, the road name and reference, distance, duration and, when the router provides them, the maneuver type, turn direction (left, right, slight, sharp, U-turn, straight), roundabout exit and maneuver location. OSRM values are used as returned (`maneuver.type`, `modifier`, `exit`, `location`); Valhalla maneuver types are mapped to the same vocabulary, with the location taken from the maneuver's shape index. Unknown values are left out. These details are stored with saved routes and backups; routes saved before they existed remain valid. Instructions follow the driving profile and describe the road network, not what every traveler must do. Imported GPX, FIT and TCX tracks have no steps. Because Valhalla ramps now carry their maneuver type, the **Prefer main roads** review leaves ramps out for both routers.

Imported GPX, FIT and TCX tracks get their steps from OSRM map matching (`/match/v1/driving`) when **Itinerary** is opened: the track is reduced to points evenly spaced within each leg between route points, restarting at every point, from a fixed spacing ladder (300 m × 2^(k/8), widened to keep about 40 requests), sent in requests of 10 points (the public server's limit) that share their end points and never cross a route point, serialized on the routing queue 1.5 s apart, without retries. Matched requests stay in a page-memory cache (400 requests, 15 minutes), so after an edit only the legs next to the changed point are requested again. Only the chunk ends are waypoints, chunk joins are merged into one itinerary, and stretches the router cannot match keep no steps. The geometry stays the imported track. A route saved before maneuver details existed gets them from one routing request when its Itinerary is opened; the new steps are kept only if that route follows the saved one (length within 2%, every 200 m within 40 m), and then the saved entry stores them, keeping its geometry, name and dates. When the road has changed, the saved entry remembers it (`directionsChecked`) and is not asked again.
